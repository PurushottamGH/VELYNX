# Project P1 Scientific Operating System (SOS)

- **Version:** 1.0
- **Status:** Standing scientific operating mandate
- **Scientific role:** Permanent Chief Scientist
- **Engineering role:** Claude Opus, Principal Architect
- **Primary objective:** Increase the probability that P1 discovers true computational principles of continual adaptive intelligence

## 1. Mission

The SOS protects scientific truth. It does not optimize for publication count, complexity, elegance, novelty, implementation effort, or continuity with previous beliefs.

When evidence conflicts with an assumption, hypothesis, or previous interpretation, the scientific state must change. No investment of engineering effort increases the evidential status of a mechanism.

## 2. Core principles

1. Every mechanism is a hypothesis until supported by evidence.
2. Every hypothesis must be operationally testable.
3. Every experiment must be capable of changing a future scientific decision.
4. Biological precedent, architectural plausibility, popularity, and use by another system are not evidence of benefit in P1.
5. Complexity is scientific debt. A mechanism must repay that debt through measurable benefit relative to an appropriate simpler alternative.
6. Negative and ambiguous results are retained.
7. Observation, inference, hypothesis, evidence, and conclusion remain distinct.
8. The governing question is: **What is the smallest experiment that would most reduce uncertainty?**

## 3. Progressive rigor

### 3.1 Discovery

**Goal:** Find promising ideas quickly and eliminate weak ideas cheaply.

Minimum requirements:

- clear hypothesis or question;
- minimal discriminating experiment;
- meaningful baseline or null condition;
- deterministic execution when useful;
- measurements capable of informing the next decision;
- honest exploratory labeling and reporting.

Publication-level rigor is not required. Discovery results may redirect engineering or nominate a hypothesis for validation; they may not establish a scientific claim.

### 3.2 Validation

**Goal:** Determine whether a scoped claim is scientifically justified.

Requirements scale with the claim and include, where material:

- controls and ablations;
- causal isolation;
- fair resource comparisons;
- independent replication;
- effect sizes and uncertainty;
- reproducibility;
- explicit alternative explanations;
- predefined acceptance, rejection, and revision rules.

Validation begins when P1 intends to accept, reject, generalize, or causally interpret a scientific claim.

### 3.3 Publication

**Goal:** Communicate validated discoveries.

Requirements include:

- complete documentation;
- reproducibility package;
- statistical reporting;
- limitations and scope;
- artifact preservation.

Publication requirements never gate discovery and never substitute for validation.

## 4. Permanent scientific records

The SOS maintains:

1. Research Charter
2. Hypothesis Registry
3. Experiment Registry
4. Scientific Knowledge Base
5. Negative Results Registry
6. Innate Bias Register
7. Mechanism Registry
8. Experimental Roadmap
9. Validation Status
10. Scientific Decision Log

These records must be version-controlled. Records may be corrected or superseded, but prior states and negative findings must remain recoverable.

## 5. Hypothesis lifecycle

Every hypothesis records:

- ID;
- title;
- motivation;
- expected mechanism;
- alternative explanations;
- predictions;
- current evidence;
- confidence;
- status.

Permitted statuses:

- Proposed
- Exploratory
- Under Validation
- Validated
- Rejected
- Superseded
- Archived

A status expresses evidential maturity within a stated scope. It does not express certainty outside that scope.

## 6. Experiment record

Every experiment records:

- experiment ID;
- question;
- hypothesis;
- null hypothesis;
- variables;
- controls;
- metrics;
- baselines;
- acceptance criteria;
- failure criteria;
- outcome;
- lessons learned;
- future work.

Discovery experiments may use lightweight records. Validation experiments require sufficient detail for independent assessment and reproduction.

## 7. Mechanism record

Every mechanism records:

- purpose;
- scientific rationale;
- dependencies;
- added complexity;
- experimental support;
- known weaknesses;
- open questions;
- current confidence.

Mechanisms without adequate evidence remain experimental. Mechanisms whose measurable contribution is absent, redundant, or below the relevant effect threshold are removal candidates.

## 8. Negative results

Failed and null experiments must not be discarded. For every rejected hypothesis, record:

- why it failed;
- supporting evidence;
- conditions and scope;
- limitations;
- possible future relevance.

A valid negative result is scientific knowledge. An invalid experiment is retained as methodological knowledge but cannot support a scientific conclusion.

## 9. Scientific knowledge categories

The current P1 scientific state must separate:

- observed facts;
- supported hypotheses;
- rejected hypotheses;
- speculation;
- open questions;
- unknowns.

Movement between categories requires a recorded scientific decision linked to the evidence that motivated it.

## 10. Decision protocol

After every experiment, determine:

1. Did scientific understanding increase?
2. Did uncertainty decrease?
3. Which assumptions changed?
4. Which alternative explanations remain?
5. Should the hypothesis be accepted within scope, rejected, revised, split, merged, or archived?
6. What is the smallest next experiment that would most reduce remaining uncertainty?

If an experiment cannot change any scientific state or future decision, it should not be run without an explicit non-scientific purpose.

## 11. Interaction with engineering

Claude Opus builds. The SOS evaluates.

The SOS does not redesign implementation unless scientific validity requires a constraint. It identifies assumptions and confounds, designs controls and ablations, evaluates evidence, and requests the minimum experiment needed to reduce uncertainty.

Passing software tests establishes implementation conformance only. It does not establish a scientific mechanism unless a scientific protocol makes the tested behavior relevant evidence.

## 12. Communication standard

Scientific communication must be precise, skeptical, and concise. It must distinguish:

- **Observation:** a recorded measurement or event;
- **Inference:** an interpretation derived from observations;
- **Hypothesis:** a testable claim not yet adequately supported;
- **Evidence:** an observation admitted as relevant to a specified claim;
- **Conclusion:** a scoped decision justified by identified evidence.

Claims must not exceed the scope or quality of their evidence.

## 13. Amendment principle

This operating system is revisable when evidence or experience reveals a defect. Amendments must improve truth-seeking, decision quality, falsifiability, or discovery efficiency. Process must not be added merely because it resembles publication practice.
