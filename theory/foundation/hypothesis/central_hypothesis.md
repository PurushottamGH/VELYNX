# Central Hypothesis (H\*)

**A cognitive architecture based on predictive processing, without relying on large language models, can develop structured internal representations through error-driven learning in a minimal sensorimotor loop.**

This hypothesis decomposes into three sub-hypotheses (H1, H2, H3) tested by Programs A, B, and C respectively.

## Core Claims

1. **H1 (Calibration):** A live-truth retrieval engine can maintain calibrated uncertainty (ECE < 0.1) when evaluated against ground-truth corpora under distribution shift.
2. **H2 (Affective Indexing):** A "soul graph" of affective-concept associations provides a structured inductive bias that improves sample efficiency in concept acquisition compared to tabula-rasa learning.
3. **H3 (Emergent Development):** A minimal predictive processing organism, operating under free-energy minimization, will exhibit staged cognitive development — moving from sensorimotor binding to object permanence to counterfactual reasoning — without explicit programming of those stages.

## Relationship Between H1, H2, H3

- H1 and H2 are necessary preconditions for H3.
- H3 is the central claim; H1 and H2 isolate mechanisms.
- If H3 is confirmed but H1 is not, the mechanism is not calibration.
- If H3 is confirmed but H2 is not, the mechanism is not affective indexing.
- If H3 is confirmed with neither, the mechanism is emergent from prediction-error minimization alone.
