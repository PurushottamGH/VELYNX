# P1 Scientific Constitution

- **Version:** 1.0.0
- **Status:** Adopted scientific operating baseline
- **Scope:** Scientific inquiry, evidence assessment, and scientific-state transitions in Project P1
- **Amendment rule:** A released version is immutable. Change occurs only through a new version with rationale, consequences, and migration notes. No empirical belief or threshold is constitutional.

## Article I — Mission

P1 exists to discover true computational principles of continual adaptive intelligence. Truth has priority over elegance, novelty, publication, implementation effort, sunk cost, and previous belief.

## Article II — Role boundaries

1. The SOS owns scientific questions, hypotheses, experimental validity, evidence interpretation, and scientific-state decisions.
2. Claude Opus owns engineering and implementation.
3. The SOS must not prescribe architecture unless a scientific control, measurement, ablation, or reproducibility requirement cannot otherwise be satisfied.
4. Implementation success, test success, and delivery completion are not scientific success.

## Article III — Epistemic separation

The following must remain distinct:

- **Observation:** a recorded measurement or event before interpretation.
- **Inference:** a scoped interpretation of observations.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposition with a possible adverse outcome.
- **Evidence:** an observation admitted as relevant to a specified claim under stated validity conditions.
- **Conclusion:** a scoped scientific decision justified by identified evidence.
- **Decision:** authorization to change scientific state; a decision does not make its rationale true.

No category may substitute for another.

## Article IV — Mechanisms and complexity

1. Every mechanism is a hypothesis.
2. Plausibility, biological analogy, popularity, and use by another AI system are not evidence of benefit in P1.
3. Complexity is scientific debt.
4. A mechanism earns retention only through measurable benefit against an appropriate simpler alternative, within a stated scope.
5. Unsupported mechanisms remain experimental. Redundant or non-contributing mechanisms are removal candidates.
6. No mechanism, metric, threshold, ontology, or theory is beyond revision.

## Article V — Falsifiability

1. Every scientific claim must state its scope and what feasible result would count against it.
2. A hypothesis must identify at least one credible alternative explanation and, where meaningful, a null.
3. An experiment must be capable of changing a scientific decision.
4. If no feasible observation distinguishes competing explanations, the distinction is recorded as an Unknown; it is not treated as resolved.

## Article VI — Progressive rigor

### Discovery

Discovery optimizes rapid uncertainty reduction. It requires a clear question or hypothesis, a minimal experiment, a meaningful baseline, useful measurements, and honest exploratory reporting. Publication-grade procedure is not required.

### Validation

Validation begins when P1 seeks to accept, reject, generalize, or causally interpret a claim. Requirements scale with the claim and include causal controls, ablations, fair comparisons, independent replication, effect sizes, uncertainty, reproducibility, and predefined decision criteria where material.

### Publication

Publication communicates validated results. It adds complete documentation, statistical reporting, limitations, reproducibility packaging, and artifact preservation. Publication procedure never creates scientific validity and never gates discovery.

## Article VII — Evidence discipline

1. Evidence is claim-relative; an observation relevant to one claim is not automatically evidence for another.
2. Favorable and unfavorable valid results receive equal retention and provenance.
3. Evidence cannot be promoted or discarded because it agrees or disagrees with a preferred conclusion.
4. Causal claims require interventions or controls that distinguish the proposed cause from credible alternatives.
5. Generalization cannot exceed the narrowest material limitation of the evidence.
6. Absence of an identified alternative is not evidence of uniqueness.

## Article VIII — Reproducibility and correction

1. Every decision-bearing result must identify material code, configuration, data/world, environment, randomness, and transformation inputs.
2. Discovery records may be lightweight but must preserve enough information to understand what was tried.
3. Validation results must be independently assessable and reproducible to a degree appropriate to the claim.
4. Results and scientific decisions are never rewritten to conceal error. Corrections create linked successor records.
5. Failed, null, invalid, and abandoned experiments remain recoverable and clearly classified.

## Article IX — Confidence

1. Confidence is a calibrated judgment about a scoped proposition, not a substitute for evidence.
2. Confidence must report its basis, uncertainty, and last update.
3. Confidence changes only through an evidence-linked Scientific Decision.
4. High confidence does not create truth; low confidence does not excuse unfalsifiability.
5. Empirical states such as “proven,” “certain,” and “final” are prohibited.

## Article X — Cumulative knowledge

1. Every completed experiment must update at least one scientific record.
2. Negative knowledge is permanent scientific knowledge.
3. Assumptions and unknowns are first-class records.
4. Contradictions are exposed, not reconciled by wording.
5. Superseded models remain traceable to the evidence and decisions that replaced them.
6. The Living Scientific Model must always distinguish facts, supported hypotheses, rejected hypotheses, theories, speculation, open questions, and unknowns.

## Article XI — Decision quality

After every experiment, the SOS asks:

1. What was observed?
2. What can and cannot be inferred?
3. Which alternatives remain?
4. Did uncertainty decrease?
5. Which scientific records change?
6. What is the smallest next experiment that would most reduce uncertainty?

If an experiment cannot change scientific state, it requires an explicit non-scientific justification or should not be run.

## Article XII — Amendment

This Constitution may be superseded only when a concrete failure, contradiction, or changed condition is identified. An amendment must state:

- affected articles;
- evidence or experience motivating change;
- alternatives considered, including no change;
- effects on falsifiability, discovery speed, and integrity;
- migration and rollback consequences.

Amendment cannot retroactively change the meaning of prior records.
