## Evidence-bearing interpretations

### `HYP-2026-0001` — No verdict, not a negative scientific result

The inspected E0 execution produced zero growth events because its trigger compared incompatible scales. This is strong evidence that the execution was invalid for testing H*. It is not evidence that H* is false.

### `HYP-2026-0002` — A failed instance does not close the achievable-system claim

The historical system emitted `CERTAIN` on a fabricated irrelevant answer. That directly rejects any statement that the tested legacy tiers were already honest or calibrated. It does not prove that a retrieval system cannot be calibrated; the formal hypothesis remains open.

### `HYP-2026-0005` — Rejection is narrow

R1 supplies a valid negative result against the tested FreeEnergyPolicy's PredictionRMSE advantage. The confidence interval is wide, so the record says “no measured advantage,” not “equivalent to null” and not “all free-energy formulations are false.”

### `HYP-2026-0006` — Benchmark inference rejected

Complete Tier-2 paraphrase collapse demonstrates that the exact-keyword benchmark did not establish robustness beyond lexical lookup. The conclusion is about the benchmark and detector, not all semantic systems.

### `HYP-2026-0007` and `HYP-2026-0008` — Descriptive M0 signal only

Seed 0 supplies a reproducible association between bundled replay variants and measured forgetting. It does not identify historical replay content or surprise timing as the cause. These hypotheses remain exploratory until matched causal controls are run.

## Explicit non-distinguishability

1. `HYP-2026-0009` and stateful `HYP-2026-0010` are not competing hypotheses as written; the latter contains the former.
2. Behavior-only evidence cannot locate a historical carrier as internal, external, or embodied memory.
3. Held-out loss alone cannot distinguish Bayesian, gradient, or selection-based update mechanisms.
4. A replay benefit without resource matching cannot distinguish historical information from extra optimization or altered decay.
